Bathroom Water Damage Help in Maricopa

Tile, tubs and fixtures are made to get wet on top, but what lies beneath them is not. When caulk cracks, grout wears thin or a toilet seal fails, a little water slips past with every shower and every flush. It collects in the subfloor and the wall behind the tile, out of sight. In Maricopa, we locate the entry point, measure how far the water has spread, then dry or take out the wet material.

Slow leaks are the norm here, which means what you find has typically been there longer, and gone further, than it seems. The tile can look perfectly fine while the subfloor beneath the toilet has gone soft clear through. Our crew maps the wet area using meters and a thermal camera so nothing extra gets torn out, and only spots that cannot dry where they are get opened.

Warning Signs

  • Inside the vanity, a base shelf that is stained, soft or out of shape
  • A hollow sound, movement, or loose tiles in the floor by the bathtub
  • Caulk along the tub or shower that is cracked, peeling, or black with mold
  • Near the tub or toilet, sheet vinyl that is lifting, curling or turning gray
  • A musty smell that comes back no matter how often the room is cleaned
  • A brown ring or sagging spot on the ceiling directly below the bathroom
How We Fix It

How We Handle Bathroom Water Damage

1

Pinpoint the Leak

Each fixture is tested separately, from the supply lines to the toilet, tub and shower, and thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went beneath tile and in the walls.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

Soft wall board, loose tile and subfloor that has rotted are removed. If the toilet was the source, we handle that section as contaminated, and porous materials we cannot clean go.

3

Dry the Hidden Spaces

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the subfloor, the wall framing and the ceiling space below. We apply antimicrobial treatment and confirm dry readings before anything is closed up.

4

Rebuild and Seal

New subfloor and backer board go in, and we can coordinate the tile, toilet seal, caulk and ceiling work, meaning the path of the leak gets closed and not just hidden.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Mold Behind Tile

By themselves, grout and tile do not keep water out. When the layer behind them stays wet, mold grows on the wall board where cleaning never reaches.

Trapped Steam

A weak or missing exhaust fan leaves steam in the room after every shower. It condenses on the ceiling and walls, encourages mold, and keeps already wet materials from drying.

Rotted Floor at the Toilet

When the wax ring gives out, a bit of water escapes every time the toilet is flushed. The toilet eventually rocks because this category 3 water has rotted the subfloor at the flange.

The Room Below

Before dripping, water runs along joists and pipes, which can put the downstairs stain several feet away from the real leak. Light fixtures and wiring can be in its path.

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

First days

Water Slips Through

Each shower pushes a little water through the failed caulk or grout. Backer board and the outer rim of the subfloor take it in, and there is still nothing to see.

The first several weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

Between showers, the wood below the tile has no time to dry out. Mold starts on the underside of the floor and inside the wall cavity.

One to three months

Signs Show Up

The caulk line goes black, the grout gets darker, and tiles work loose. Where a room sits underneath, its ceiling develops a stain that gradually spreads.

Six months or more

Structure Weakens

Around the tub or toilet the subfloor goes soft, and the joists may start rotting. At this point the job means new tile and carpentry on top of the drying.
