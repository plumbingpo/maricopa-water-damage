How Much Does Water Damage Cost in Maricopa?

Home / Blog / How Much Does Water Damage Cost in Maricopa?

The Short Answer

Typical Maricopa water damage projects cost from $1,500 up to $10,000 or more. A minor job involving clean water may cost less than roughly $1,500. Major flooding or a sewage backup that needs rebuilding can run well above $10,000.

These are typical national ranges, not a quote. Pricing in Maricopa, AZ depends on local wages and the going rate for materials, so your number may differ. The only way to get an exact figure is an on-site inspection with moisture readings.

Pricing by Category of Water

Restoration pricing usually starts with the type of water involved. Contamination level places every loss in one of three industry categories. The dirtier the water, the more protective gear, cleaning and material removal the job needs.

Across the US, cleanup tends to run roughly $3 to $7.50 per square foot depending on category. Typical figures look like this.

CategoryWhat it isTypical cost per sq ft
Category 1, clean waterSupply lines, faucets, water heater tanksRoughly $3 to $4
Category 2, gray waterWashing machine or dishwasher discharge, sump pump failuresAbout $4 to $6.50
Category 3, black waterStorm surge, flooding rivers, sewageRoughly $7 to $7.50

The category is not fixed. After about two days of sitting, water that started clean carries bacteria and is reclassified to gray or black. It is part of why delay makes a job more expensive.

What Drives the Price

Two homes with the same broken pipe can get very different bills. When a job is priced, the contractor considers the following.

  • Area affected. More wet square footage means more labor, more equipment and more days on site.
  • Water category. Handling is least expensive when the water is clean. Sewage is the most expensive.
  • How long it sat. Water spreads into subfloors and wall cavities over time. Catching a leak within an hour is much cheaper than discovering it when you return from a weekend away.
  • Materials. Tile and concrete dry well. Hardwood, carpet pad, drywall and insulation often need special drying or replacement.
  • Mold. If growth has started, mold remediation is added to the scope, with containment and air filtration.
  • Reconstruction. One part of the bill is drying. Replacing drywall, flooring, cabinets and paint afterward is a separate one, and on large jobs it is often the bigger of the two.

Estimates usually cover a pair of phases, extraction of the water and then drying of the structure. In general the drying phase lasts 3 to 5 days.

Many companies charge for each day the air movers and dehumidifiers run. A home that dries in three days costs less than one that takes six.

Typical Costs for Common Jobs

These are typical national ranges for the jobs we see most often in Maricopa. Use the numbers to get oriented and not as a firm price.

  • Burst pipe. Cleaning up and drying out often runs $1,500 to $5,000, with repair of the burst pipe billed in addition. Expect more if it ran for hours or soaked more than one floor.
  • Appliance leak. Catch a leaking water heater, washing machine or dishwasher early and the bill is often $1,000 to $4,000. Slow leaks under cabinets cost more because of hidden damage.
  • Basement flood. The usual range is $2,000 to $10,000 or more, varying with the water's source, its depth and any finished surfaces. Finished basements land near the top of the range for basement water removal.
  • Sewage backup. Many jobs cost $3,000 to $10,000, some more. Because sewage cleanup falls under Category 3, crews disinfect the whole area and take out porous materials.
  • Roof leak. Interior work on wet ceilings, walls and insulation often comes to $1,000 to $5,000, and the roof fix is extra.

Will Insurance Pay for Water Damage in Maricopa?

Two things decide it: the cause of the loss and your policy's wording. Standard homeowners policies often cover damage that is sudden and accidental, such as a pipe that bursts or a supply line that fails without warning.

A leak that developed slowly is usually excluded. Months of dripping from a fitting may be labeled a maintenance issue by the insurer. Unless you have a separate flood policy, water that enters from outside, like storm surge or a rising river, is usually excluded. Sewer backup often needs its own add-on.

Before assuming anything, read the policy or phone your agent. The deductible is a factor as well. On a small job, the repair may cost less than the deductible, and a claim may not make sense.

How to Keep the Bill Down

What happens early, within the first couple of days, determines the bulk of a water damage bill. Some basic actions go a long way.

  • Act fast. Water keeps spreading until it is removed. Every hour adds wet material.
  • Shut off the water. Know where your main valve is and close it as soon as you find a leak.
  • Document everything. Before moving or discarding anything, record it in photos and on video. Keep receipts.
  • Call a pro before mold starts. Mold can begin growing in 24 to 48 hours. After that point, expect added time and added cost.
  • Move what you can. Get rugs, furniture and boxes off wet floors if it is safe.

Do not walk through standing water if the power is on, and stay out of anything that may contain sewage. No saving is worth a shock or an infection. If the water came from outside, leave flood cleanup to a crew with the right protective gear.

Getting a Real Number

A national range helps with budgeting, yet no house is the average house. An inspection with a moisture meter shows how far the water travelled and what it will take to dry and repair.

Damage only gets bigger with time, so call for restoration of water damage in Maricopa, AZ and have the inspection done now.
