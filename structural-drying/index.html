Of all the stages in restoring water damage, drying the structure counts most, and it is the stage most often botched by people without training. After extraction removes the standing water, significant moisture remains trapped in structural materials, including drywall, wood framing, subflooring, concrete, insulation and other building components. Controlled evaporation and dehumidification, applied scientifically, are needed to get that moisture out before a wet structure develops mold, rotting wood, degraded materials and odors.

Psychrometry is the science of how air temperature, humidity and moisture interact, and the IICRC-certified technicians who dry structures for Maricopa Restoration know it well. That knowledge lets them work out where equipment goes, what temperature to hold and how air should move so evaporation is as effective as possible on every job. Different materials (drywall, hardwood, concrete, plaster) dry at different rates and need different approaches to reach acceptable moisture levels without causing additional damage.

Our structural drying process includes daily moisture monitoring with calibrated moisture meters, thermo-hygrometers and thermal imaging cameras. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Each day we use those numbers to reposition or retune the equipment so drying stays efficient. A surface that feels dry to your hand proves nothing, so the job is finished only once all affected materials test within the acceptable standards set by IICRC S500.

How Our Structural Drying Works

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle structural drying from start to finish:

1

Moisture Assessment

We build a complete moisture map of every affected area with hygrometers, thermal imaging and moisture meters. From that map we decide where equipment goes and record our baseline readings.

2

Equipment Deployment

Our crew sets up high-velocity air movers along with commercial LGR units in the positions the moisture map calls for. The materials being dried determine how we set the airflow and the temperature.

3

Daily Checks and Adjustments

Each day a technician visits to document progress, reposition equipment and measure moisture at every monitoring point. We keep you posted on how the timeline looks.

4

Dry Standard Verification

When moisture readings reach acceptable levels throughout all affected materials, we document final readings, remove equipment and certify that your property has reached dry standard.

Warning Signs for Structural Drying

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Beads of moisture on windows and cold surfaces after the water event
  • Wallboard with water stains, puffiness or a spongy feel
  • Signs of deterioration where an old leak was left to dry on its own
  • Gaps opening up in a hardwood floor, or planks with raised edges or centers
  • Wall finishes that start to peel or bubble in the days after water damage
  • Humidity that will not come down in the affected space, whatever ventilation you try
  • No visible water left, yet walls or floors still feel cold and damp

Why Hire a Professional for Structural Drying?

Drying a structure properly takes science, and guesswork has no place in it. Simply placing fans and hoping for the best is the most common cause of secondary mold damage after water events. Professional structural drying requires understanding how different materials absorb and release moisture, calculating the grain depression (moisture removal capacity) each situation needs, and monitoring daily to confirm drying is progressing properly. Lacking that understanding, people either stop too soon and leave moisture for mold, or dry too hard and harm wood and other materials.

Consumer products and the equipment professionals dry structures with have very little in common. High-velocity air movers produce precise airflow patterns that speed up evaporation from the structure, and low-grain refrigerant (LGR) commercial dehumidifiers capture 10 to 20 times the moisture a home unit can. Between the investment in equipment and the training needed to run it, it is no surprise that DIY drying falls well short of a professional job.

Structural Drying Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • New odors or wet areas during the drying period should be reported to your technician right away
  • Avoid moving the machines yourself, because their placement follows our moisture map
  • The dehumidifiers and air movers should run around the clock until our technician takes them out
  • Air movers need a clear path, so avoid stacking things on the equipment or in front of it
  • Daily monitoring only works if someone can let the technician into the property
  • Go by the meter, not by touch, since a dry-feeling surface may still hold moisture deep inside
